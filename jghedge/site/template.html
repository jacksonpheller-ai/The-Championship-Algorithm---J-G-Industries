How the desk works

Every day the engine pulls the slate, injury report, DraftKings lines and player props, rebuilds every team and player model from games already played, simulates each game 20,000 times and prices every market. It then does something most betting tools do not: it decides whether tonight is worth betting at all, and says so.

The nightly verdict

The night score is the shrunk expected profit of the reference ticket ($100 bankroll, $0.50 units) as a fraction of bankroll, counted only over bets that survive the growth gate. PASS below 0.05% (half of a median betting night): nothing on the board grows a bankroll at these sizes, and skipping costs no expected growth while cutting variance. LIGHT from 0.05% to 0.15%: the ticket is shown at the default Kelly fraction only. BET at 0.15% and above: the full ticket, with the opt-in fraction available. Three flags modify it: a line that has moved past our number (skip that bet), the props regime (stand down, lean under, neutral) and lineup uncertainty (games with game-time decisions among the starters). The banner says what would flip the verdict, so you know what to watch before tip.

Shrinkage: betting half the edge the model claims

Across 821 settled replay bets the model predicted +9.7% and realised +6.6%; on totals alone, +6.3% predicted against +3.4% realised. The desk therefore prices every total and parlay as if the true edge were half the model's (s = 0.5, re-estimated each Monday from both replay seeds) and gives props zero weight (s = 0): fitted on one season and tested on the next, the prop model made the market's price worse, not better. Every number you see on the ticket, from the win probability to the stake, is the shrunk one; the raw figure sits beneath it.

Sizing tiers and the growth gate

Stakes are whole units. The Kelly fraction depends on how many units the bankroll holds: under 25 units ("tiny") the bank cannot express a sensible stake and the desk asks for a smaller unit; 25 to 100 units ("small") bets singles and two-leg parlays at 0.35× Kelly on the shrunk edge; 100 units and above ("standard") uses 0.25×, with 0.5× as an opt-in that roughly doubles the chance of a 50% drawdown in a season (about 13% versus about 0%). No bet exceeds 5% of the bankroll and no night exceeds 10%. Before anything is staked it must pass the growth gate: one unit of it must raise the expected log of the bankroll at the shrunk probability. Bmin on the ticket is the smallest bankroll at which one unit of that bet passes; below it the bet is a coin flip with a fee, however good the edge looks.

Two platforms, two jobs

Fliff takes the game bets: totals and cross-game parlays of totals, where the replayed edge lives. Each row carries our fair line and the worst line to accept at -110 ("Over 228.5 or better; skip above 229.5"), because the number you are offered will differ from the number we priced. PrizePicks takes the player legs. A pick-em slip is not a bet against a price; it is a fixed payout multiple for getting k of n picks right, so the only thing that matters is the probability of each leg at the posted line. The desk anchors each player's simulated distribution to the DraftKings market median, reads P(more) and P(less) at the PrizePicks line (or at the DraftKings line, flagged as a proxy, when PrizePicks is unreachable), shrinks the edge by half, and evaluates slips against the break-even probability of each payout table. The slip builder computes the exact hit-count distribution of any combination from 2,000 joint simulation draws, so correlated legs (same player, same game) are priced correctly rather than assumed independent. The payout table and minimum entry are assumptions until confirmed in the app; both are editable.

Data points

  • Team efficiency: offensive and defensive rating per 100 possessions from every box score this season, adjusted for opponent and home court by weighted ridge regression with a 45-day half-life. Pace modelled the same way.
  • Four factors (eFG%, turnover rate, offensive rebound rate, free-throw rate, both ends), SRS, strength of schedule and true shooting from Basketball-Reference.
  • Player value: Box Plus/Minus split into offensive and defensive BPM, blended with each player's individual Offensive Rating and Defensive Rating. This season and last are blended by minutes.
  • Injuries: ESPN statuses become play probabilities; the change in expected minutes times each player's value above replacement moves the team's ratings. Returning players get reduced minutes by games missed.
  • Schedule: rest days, back-to-backs, three games in four nights, travel miles, time-zone shifts and altitude (capped; a Utah effect, not a general one). Late in the season (70+ games played) totals lean over by about two points, the one totals feature that held in every season tested.
  • Market: moneyline, spread and total with prices, de-vigged, plus opening lines and a log of where the line was when we priced it.
  • Dropped: referee crews, following or fading line moves, same-game prop parlays, and a dozen totals features that did not survive out of sample.

Parlays

Parlays only combine legs from different games, so their odds of hitting are the product of the legs' shrunk probabilities, computed exactly. The cards are ranked by expected log growth of one unit, not by expected value: at a small bankroll a +500 parlay with a large stated edge is far more growth-negative than a -110 total with a small one.

What the replay can and cannot test

The replay re-runs the whole pipeline on every past night at that morning's line with only information available before tip, including the integer-unit sizing at several bankrolls, and it is run with two random seeds because a single season swings by $100 or more on a $1,000 bankroll from the draws alone. It cannot test the props market (no clean historical prop prices), PrizePicks payouts (no history of their lines), line shopping across books, or how much of the opener's value survives to the late-morning run. The growth plan on the Tonight tab bootstraps 160-night seasons from those replayed nights at your bankroll and unit; treat its tails as the honest range and its median as optimistic.

House rules

  • Bet the number shown or better. If the line has moved past the max line, skip the bet.
  • Lines come from one book. Shopping the same bet across books is the cheapest edge there is.
  • Late scratches change everything. The desk refreshes before tip; check the injury report and the lineup flag.
  • Passing is a position. A night with no bet is not a night the desk failed.
  • Only bet money you can afford to lose. Betting is legal only where licensed and for adults of legal age. Help: 1-800-GAMBLER.
