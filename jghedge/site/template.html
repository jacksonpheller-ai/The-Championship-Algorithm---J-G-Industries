How the desk works

Every night the engine pulls the slate, injury report, DraftKings lines and player props, rebuilds every team and player model from scratch using only games already played, simulates each game 20,000 times and sizes bets on the joint outcomes.

Data points

  • Team efficiency: offensive and defensive rating per 100 possessions from every box score this season, adjusted for opponent and home court by weighted ridge regression, with recent games weighted more (45-day half-life). Pace modelled the same way.
  • Four factors (eFG%, turnover rate, offensive rebound rate, free-throw rate, on both ends), SRS, strength of schedule and true shooting from Basketball-Reference.
  • Player value: Box Plus/Minus split into offensive and defensive BPM, blended with each player's individual Offensive Rating (points produced per 100 possessions, weighted by usage) and individual Defensive Rating (points allowed per 100 while on court). Values blend this season and last by minutes; PER, TS%, usage, WS/48 and VORP are shown on every rotation.
  • Roster-talent prior: each team starts the season from last year's ratings regressed to the mean, blended with the minutes-weighted BPM of the players it is actually using now.
  • Injuries: ESPN injury statuses become play probabilities (Out 0%, Doubtful 15%, Questionable 50%, Probable 90%); the change in expected minutes times each player's BPM above replacement moves the team's offensive and defensive ratings.
  • Schedule: rest days, back-to-backs, three games in four nights, travel miles between arenas, time-zone shifts and altitude (Denver, Utah, Mexico City). Coefficients fit from three seasons of results.
  • Elo: a margin-of-victory Elo rating as an independent second opinion, blended with the efficiency model.
  • Market: moneyline, spread and total with prices, de-vigged (power method for moneylines), plus opening lines.
  • Props: each player's per-minute rates (this season and last), projected minutes, vacated minutes and usage from absent teammates, the opponent's allowed rate for that stat, game pace and blowout risk. Stats are drawn from negative binomial distributions inside the game simulation, so combos and same-game legs stay correlated.

From projection to price

The model's margin and total are blended with the market's. The weight was fit out of sample: about 25–30% model against a morning line, falling to about 5% close to tip, because closing lines are hard to beat. Outcomes are simulated from the empirical distribution of past errors rather than a bell curve, which prices big underdogs correctly. Prop probabilities come from a logistic model fit on real pre-game prop results.

Sizing

Bets need at least 1.5% expected value and an edge under 12 points of probability (bigger gaps usually mean a stale line or late news). Parlays combine legs from different games only and are settled exactly as books do (pushes shrink the parlay). Stakes come from a simultaneous Kelly optimiser over all candidates, then scaled by the risk profile and capped per bet and per night.

House rules

  • Bet the number shown or better. If a line has moved past it, rerun the slate or skip the bet.
  • Lines come from one book. Shopping the same bet across books is the cheapest edge available.
  • Late scratches change everything. The site refreshes before tip; check the injury report.
  • Only bet money you can afford to lose. Sports betting is legal only where it is licensed and for adults of legal age. Help: 1-800-GAMBLER.
