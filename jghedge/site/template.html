    How it works

    The desk does one thing: it finds bets that win more often than their price assumes, and tells you how much of tonight's amount to put on each. Most nights that is very little, and some nights it is nothing.

    1. What happens each day

    The desk is scheduled to run every two hours. In the evening it prices the next day's slate at the opening lines, where the totals edge has been largest; through the day it re-prices as lines and injury news move. Each run pulls the schedule, the injury report, and DraftKings' lines and player props through ESPN, rebuilds every team and player rating from games already played, simulates every game 20,000 times, prices every bet and grades any finished night. GitHub, which runs the schedule, often starts jobs hours late, so the Tonight tab says how old the lines are and warns when they are stale. Games that have tipped drop off the page by your device's clock.

    2. Where the edge comes from

    • Game totals, on Fliff. Our points projection disagrees with the posted total early enough (before the line settles) to beat the price. This is the edge with a track record: the whole method has been replayed on three full past seasons using only what was known before each game.
    • Player picks, on PrizePicks. PrizePicks pays the same multiple whatever the odds. When a sportsbook prices one side of a player line at, say, −178, that side hits about 60% of the time, but PrizePicks pays it like any other pick. We start from the sportsbook's price, correct it for the one bias we can measure (overs hit less often than priced, so "Less" is where most of the edge is), and look for PrizePicks lines that are as easy or easier.

    Everything else is priced but not bet. Spreads and moneylines lost money in two of three replayed seasons. Our own player projections, tested on a season they hadn't seen, made the sportsbook's price worse, so they are shown for reference and never used.

    3. How a game total is priced

    1. Team ratings. Points scored and allowed per 100 possessions, and pace, from every box score this season, weighted toward recent games and adjusted for opponent and home court.
    2. Who is playing. Each player's value comes from Box Plus/Minus and their individual offensive and defensive ratings. The injury report becomes a chance of playing, and the missing minutes move the team's ratings.
    3. Schedule. Rest, back-to-backs, three games in four nights, travel, time zones and altitude.
    4. Blend with the market. Our total counts for 30% six or more hours before tip, falling to 0% at tip; the rest is the market's number. Late in the season (teams 70+ games in) 1.2 points are added (2.5 in the last few games or with a tanking underdog), the one totals pattern that held on seasons it wasn't fit on.
    5. Simulate. 20,000 games per matchup, using how far real games have landed from the projection, give the chance of the over and the under at the posted line.
    6. Safety cut and the floor. The edge is halved (section 5). A total is a bet when its edge is still at least 1.5% per $1 after the cut.

    4. How a player pick is priced

    1. Each player's minutes and stat line are simulated inside the game simulation (pace, blowouts, who else is out).
    2. That spread of outcomes is re-centred on DraftKings' line and price, so at DraftKings' line our chance starts from the sportsbook's own chance with the bookmaker's margin removed.
    3. The under lean. Sportsbook overs hit less often than their price says. On 8,009 past DraftKings lines they hit 3.4 points less (2.8 in 2025–26 and 4.9 so far in 2026–27; the same direction in every month and every stat), and correcting for it on one season improved the forecasts on the other. So 3.0 points are moved from More to Less before a pick is priced. The figure blends that history with the desk's own log of this season's lines. A More pick the sportsbook prices at 59% is treated as about 56%; a Less pick at 59% as about 62%.
    4. The chance is then read at PrizePicks' line. When PrizePicks' board can't be read (it blocks the server most nights), DraftKings' line stands in: compare with the app, or type the app's line and the page re-prices the pick.
    5. The chance is compared with the break-even: 57.7% a pick for a 2-pick Power, about 54% for a 5- or 6-pick Flex.
    6. Slips: the chance of each number of hits is counted from the joint simulations, so two picks from the same game are priced together rather than assumed independent.

    Evidence: on 2,866 DraftKings prop lines that moved between open and close, this method (priced at the old line, anchored to the new price) predicted 60.7% for the favoured side, which hit 59.0%. Picks it put at 60–65% hit 66% (589 picks). There is no history of PrizePicks lines, so PrizePicks results are being tracked from scratch on the Record tab.

    "Clear take" on a pick is the line at which our side reaches 60%. When PrizePicks' board can't be read, the desk only suggests a slip if every pick reaches its clear-take line in the app.

    5. The safety cut

    Across 821 replayed bets the model expected +9.7% and made +6.6%; on totals it expected +6.3% and made +3.4%. So every edge is cut in half before anything is decided. A total the model gives 56% at −110 (which needs 52.4%) is treated as 54.2%. PrizePicks picks are cut halfway toward their break-even the same way. Every chance on this page is the cut one.

    6. The night's call

    The desk scores the night by the cut expected profit of its own ticket ($100 bankroll, 50¢ bets, a quarter of the Kelly stake). Below 0.05% it is a pass: nothing earns its risk, and the desk stakes nothing on games even if one total still shows an edge (the row says so). The replay backs this: totals bet on the thinnest betting nights (a score of 0.05–0.10%) returned about −3% per $1 over 280 replayed bets (two random seeds), against about +13% on bet nights, so a night thinner still is not worth chasing. From 0.05% to 0.15% it is a light night: thin edges. From 0.15% it is a bet night.

    7. How your nightly amount is split

    1. PrizePicks first. If a slip has an edge and PrizePicks' $5 minimum entry is no more than half your amount, it gets the minimum entry. Only the minimum, because PrizePicks edges have no track record yet.
    2. Fliff with the rest. All of it on a bet night, half on a light night, none on a pass. It is split across the night's bets in the same proportions as the desk's own ticket, so larger and surer edges get more, in steps of Fliff's $0.25 minimum.
    3. You keep whatever isn't used.

    The desk doesn't know your bankroll and doesn't need it, but it can't protect it either. Keep the nightly amount to a small slice of the money you've set aside for this (a tenth or less), so a bad night can't do much damage. Expect losing nights: the edges are a few percent.

    Words on this page

    Our chance
    How often we expect the bet to win, after the safety cut.
    Needs
    How often the bet has to win to break even at its price: 52.4% at −110; for PrizePicks, the slip's break-even per pick.
    Edge
    Expected profit per $1, after the cut. +2% means $2 a $100 in the long run, with big swings night to night.
    Our line
    The total at which we think over and under are equally likely.
    Worst line
    The worst number at which a total is still a bet: the highest line for an over, the lowest for an under. Past it, skip.
    Clear take
    For a PrizePicks pick, the line at which our side reaches 60%.
    Stand-in line
    DraftKings' line shown in place of PrizePicks' when PrizePicks' board couldn't be read. Check the app.
    Power / Flex
    Power pays only if every pick hits; Flex pays less but still pays with one (or two) misses.
    Bet / Light / Pass
    The night's call (section 6): how much of your amount goes to game bets.

    House rules

    • Bet the number shown or better. If the line has moved past the worst line, skip it.
    • Lines come from one book. The same bet at a better number elsewhere is free edge.
    • Late scratches change totals. Check the injury report, or wait for the later runs.
    • A night with no bet is not a failure. Passing costs nothing.
    • Only bet money you can afford to lose. Legal only where licensed and for adults of legal age. Help: 1-800-GAMBLER.
